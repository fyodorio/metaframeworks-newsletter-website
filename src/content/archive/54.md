[🎶 _Every day for us something new, open mind for a different view, and nothing else matters..._ 🎶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tAGnKpE4NCI&list=PLYRq_7Yox1jDETeL_YgKUc8DXduCV9jA2&index=55)

It was a hell of a decade, the last one — from 2016 to 2026. Not only globally but even personally, if anyone looks back at the time they had during these years, they'll find a lot of bad and good things. Maybe more of the former but still. For web development with frameworks and metaframeworks, which is the focus of this newsletter, this decade was groundbreaking (*compare it to [the 70 years of Fortran](https://fortran-lang.org/news/2026/09-15-Fortran-birthday/) and realize how different the numbers and outcomes are!*). Is it the peak of it? Is it the end of the good ol' JavaScript dance on the cornerstones of the web platform? Let's find out by looking at some highlights of the passed four weeks and ruminating on some memories floated up by the news of them.

## The Good

The realization of this decade and the significance of it came to me with some news about Svelte and Nuxt celebrating their 10 year anniversary this month. But with all my love to both of them, if you start going down the memory lane, the October of 2016 and 2016th itself were much more than that.

2016 was the year of Angular 2 wars. It was the year of Vue 2 which changed the acceptance of developer experience forever. It was the year of create-react-app and tools that could make CRA almighty — wide Redux adoption, yarn as a robust npm alternative, Electron 1.0, GraphQL to name a few (*so basically, kind of the year of metaframeworkization of the ecosystem!*).  Even the cross-platform .NET Core 1.0 (*which I personally hate but still can't deny importance of*). On top of that, it was the year which made ES6, service workers, and PWA a state of the art in the web development and laid out the foundation for what we have now with really minimal changes. We even started to get things like Preact (*which has just got their [first major update](https://preactjs.com/blog/preact-11) in seven years — such stable it is!*) popularized to wide extent as a replacement for bloated ecosystem tooling, which made the shift to performance-oriented future possible, including, tangentially but still, things like Vite.

All in all, that's a lot to re-digest, re-visit thoughtfully and give thanks too (*or feel strong PTSD about, you do you*). Today's world of JavaScript tooling is something much more boring — unfortunately or not. But in many parts, the developer experience we got throughout these ten years is not bad at all.

## The Bad

The things these days start to move backwards slightly. From flourishing and opinionated takes, to generalization and consolidation. One of the examples is multiple acquisitions of the latest times, including for instance the latest news about [Deno becoming a part of Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/deno-joins-cloudflare/) (*reads like "good JS runtimes do not matter this much anymore"*). In the same vein, some (*coughs *ignorant* *coughs) people, even having discussed that with about half a dozen of authoritative peers and being a professional DevRel (*which may explain things though, I agree*), say [frameworks don't matter much anymore](https://brookslybrand.com/posts/do-frameworks-matter-anymore/). One other thing that made me cry too being added to this box became [the talk of Ryan Carniato at this month's ViteConf](https://youtu.be/PfyIEgLzNYI?si=ipLFjdWedSNWhWAP&t=17812) (*I highly recomment the other talks too, many of them just as nice and nostalgic as this decade's celebration we do itself*) which kinda burried SolidStart (*and, let's accept it, hinted to burriing the idea of other metaframeworks too*) alive in favour of `vite.config.ts`. Ryan wasn't the first one with that, as I figured the Qwik team is getting rid of their Qwik City (*their metaframework thingy*) [in favour of Qwik Router](https://next.qwik.dev/docs/qwikrouter/) in the newest framework version. This idea was probably popularized even earlier by React Router with their framework mode (*which got some updates in [the latest new version 8.5](https://github.com/remix-run/react-router/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md#v850), by the way*) instead of the actual React-Router-based Remix metaframework (*which is coming back though, and [cooking something cool](https://github.com/remix-run/remix/releases/tag/remix%403.1.0) too!*).

Which doesn't stop curious people from creating new metaframeworks too, actually — like [the new Svelte thing](https://mochi.fast/) for those who doesn't accept SvelteKit as something one could call "sufficient" for the job. Well, as them JS-framework haters say, that could be worse... For instance, [those slopware trinkets](https://blog.cloudflare.com/typescript-rust-port/) from the very aforementioned Cloudflare, huh? (*Though in fairness, maybe they buy all these projects lately to make their hackwork look better on the market, who knows...*)

But with all of that, we shouldn't forget that a metaframeworks is actually the robust harness (*in many meanings*), the well-integrated exoskeleton which leads us to better and more solid and safe future, on contrary to what many surfaced opinions state. We're not there yet though, that's for sure, even looking at the stable flood of expositional [Next.js vulnerabilities](https://nextjs.org/blog/nextjs-security-update-october-14-2026) or [multiple Vite threats](https://bsky.app/profile/vite.dev/post/3mxo6ncuxrs24), but the integrated tooling gives the dev teams a good prop for proactive measures, as opposed to scattered set of tooling pieces, and that's something to not forget about.

## The Noteworthy

The updates of the last month in the metaframeworks community are quite abundant to get too deep into but there are some important release which we'd make a crime if we wouldn't mention them today.

SvelteKit team released new long-anticipated [major version 3.0](https://bsky.app/profile/vite.dev/post/3mxo6ncuxrs24) (*with lots of "Vite-ification" related updates, and the following [bunch of important minor fixes](https://github.com/sveltejs/kit/releases/tag/%40sveltejs/kit%403.1.0)*). The guys prepare to [the annual SvelteSummit](https://sveltesummit.com) and invite everyone to celebrate the new release with them in beautiful Ljubljana. 

But if custom DSLs is not something of your type, you can check out TSX-enabled new minor version of Next.js by [reading about that](https://nextjs.org/blog/next-16-4) on the team's newly revamped and redesigned website (*from the number of the blogpost authors comparable with the number of SvelteKit users probably*). It brings new feature of Cache Components, gifts for your greedy agents, and lots of minor DX improvements (*and experiments!*), as usual.

And if one triangle there is not enough for you, you'll definitely be satisfied with (and pleased with countless technical improvements and new experimental features from) double-triangle news — [the new minor version of Nuxt](https://nuxt.com/blog/v4-6).

As we discussed already, SolidStart gives us a farewell (*probably*) with their latest minor (and quite huge) [release 2.1](https://github.com/solidjs/solid-start/releases/tag/%40solidjs/start%402.1.0). At the same time, another start, the Tanstack Start, [gets to stable release](https://tanstack.com/blog/tanstack-start-1-0) eventually, with the accompanying deep philosophical writeup which you should definitely check out if this newsletter issue has touched your nerves a bit.

Astro delivers it's [new minor version 7.4](https://astro.build/blog/astro-740/) with prevalingly technical updates and fixes, while AnalogJS is full-steam coming to new major version 3 with the corresponding out-of-the-press [migration guide](https://v3.analogjs.org/docs/guides/migrating-v2-to-v3). 

Inferno tags long-awaited [juvenile version 10](https://github.com/infernojs/inferno/releases/tag/v10.0.0) after 2 years since the previous major update (*or even 4 if you consider that version 9 was a kind of maintenance release*). The framework switched to the compiler approach (*as many folks do these days*) and makes a lot adjacent shifts for that.

Last but not least, RedwoodSDK, if you look at their release notes for the recent releases and [the latest 1.7.5](https://github.com/redwoodjs/sdk/releases/tag/v1.7.5), starts to feel a bit stagnated, while Quasar on contrary moves forward full-steam even through the minor releases [like 2.34.0](https://github.com/quasarframework/quasar/releases/tag/quasar-v2.34.0).

So that's the everchanging life in the world of metaframeworks and tools around them in the shiny and warm October of 2026. It's like the innovation boom started ten years ago had never slowed down since, no matter what new industry shifts the reality (*or future*) would bring us (*including the frameworks funerals, and web funerals, and development funerals, and all that silly whistle jazz*). I see a lot of good stuff still, a lot of good people who bring new ideas and solutions, and lot of ideas, in me and you (yes, you!) to make the world spin even without rotation tokens or whatever they put into the furnaces these days. And while we all are here, it's worth to have it spinning.
