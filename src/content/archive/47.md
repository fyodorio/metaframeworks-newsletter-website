## The Good

On a positive side of things we have two interesting articles about the aforementioned performance bets that allow to dig deeper into some personal experience around metaframeworks and technologies that drive them up.

Dennis Brotzky wrote [a beautiful (_probably even too beautiful_) blogpost](https://performance.dev/the-conductor-rewrite) in a form of insights from an interview with one of the founders of [Conductor](https://www.conductor.build) about their experience of [vibe-]migrating the codebase from `react-router` to `@tanstack/react-router`. There's a lot of technical excellence in the decision and the process around, including the very fact the described technologies easily find their way not only to web development but also to cross-platform desktop development with Tauri, engaging a lot of corresponding runtime specifics and debugging hurdles.

> We've re-built Conductor from scratch to make it twice as fast. Creating tabs, switching workspaces, and rendering files are all 50% faster, memory usage is lower, and the app is 150 MB smaller.

Sankalpa Acharya wrote about the other side of the metaframeworks toolbox — [server-client boundary constructing during application build process](https://inside-react.vercel.app/blog/how-react-server-component-integrate-with-bundler).

> One interesting thing here is that React does not expose the exact same implementation to both environments. When we build an RSC app, we are effectively building for two different runtimes.

[The source code example](https://github.com/sankalpaacharya/drop) the author refers to is quite interesting by itself being a kind of metaframework based on Rspack (_not Vite!_) which is surprising and enticing by itself.

## The Bad

Talking about bundlers and such stuff: one of the biggest go-to ecosystems for building metaframeworks on top of, Vite, along with all the projects and (_rather through all_) the contributors around it, went under new governance, specifically Cloudflare, as a part of [the VoidZero acquisition deal](https://voidzero.dev/posts/voidzero-cloudflare). It wasn't a big surprise for many people as it was a predictable direction for the OSS-based venture-backed company lead by the Vue and Vite creator and supported by the brilliant team of OSS stars. Of course, it brings some EvilCorp discourse, as many such acquisitions, but eventually, the hope is the acquired sustainability for the most important pillar of the metaframeworks ecosystem, so that's something we should focus our attention at. Rita Kozlov and Steve Faulkner of Cloudflare [talked a lot about the process](https://www.devtools.fm/episode/174) and new direction for the team at devtoolsFM podcast just recently and share a lot of insights about the way the company works with OSS these days. Another good thing is that Cloudflare being big on security will definitely help Vite to [face vulnerability threats](https://voidzero.dev/posts/whats-new-may-2026#vite-1) popping up in the ecosystem almost on a permanent basis, even though almost all the tools are rewritten to airtight Rust.

But you know what else is often written in Rust? Right, cool new malware [like Shai-Hulud and the gang](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-ironworm-malware-hits-36-packages-in-npm-supply-chain-attack/). Rust and JavaScript/Node are wonderfully connected in this regard for some reason, with `npm` being a constant (_and easy_) target for adversaries. GitHub constantly tries to improve that and in v12 `npm` [will have good new security provisions](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-06-09-upcoming-breaking-changes-for-npm-v12/) borrowed from `pnpm` so there's some hope the situation will improve, but there are just so many moving parts...

For instance, tools like [Nuxt](https://bsky.app/profile/nuxt.com/post/3mncsulo4n52b) and [Vitest](https://bsky.app/profile/vitest.dev/post/3mnan6j5ujk2g) proceed to process new multiple vulnerability reports time and time again, with some findings being critical. Check out your `package.json` on a regular basis, that's a brave new world of tech security fatigue.

From this point of view, the researchers like Rachid Allam look like new knights of the open-source metaframeworks world, working deeply inside the stack and publishing [their thoughtful and insightful findings](https://zhero-web-sec.github.io/research-and-things/re-cache-excessive-reflection-type-confusion-and-0-click-sxss-on-nextjs) which eventually benefit not only the target technology but a wider webdev community.

> Although the vulnerability was exploited in Next.js, which, as we will see, met all the conditions for reliable exploitation, it stems from an unusual mistake: mirroring request headers into response headers.

And Next.js, being a complex and multi-faceted construction, by itself has a lot of pains and gains to bring, unfortunately. New victims like Shubhra Pokhariya share [their pains and gains](https://dev.to/shubhradev/nextjs-16-broke-my-app-in-4-places-and-none-of-them-threw-an-error-51mn) related to recent upgrades, and old victims like the guys from Codepen share [their Stockholm syndrome aftermath](https://blog.codepen.io/2026/06/02/427-next-js-and-the-journey-of-ssr/) related to SSR.

All in all, isn't this a fertile ground for research?

## The Noteworthy

Release notes of popular metaframework repositories were also quite fertile for updates the last couple of weeks.

Svelte world brought a [lot of cool updates](https://svelte.dev/blog/whats-new-in-svelte-june-2026) but the most anticipated set of news lies on [the releases page of SvelteKit](https://github.com/sveltejs/kit/releases), of course, where the version 3 train coming to its official publishing date at full steam.

More or less the same goes on Astro side with [preparations for coming version 7](https://v7.docs.astro.build/en/guides/upgrade-to/v7/) and corresponding [breaking changes](https://v7.docs.astro.build/en/guides/upgrade-to/v7/).

Analog team published [new minor 2.6.0](https://github.com/analogjs/analog/releases/tag/v2.6.0) with support for [new Angular 22](https://angular.dev/events/v22). The final thing for today is the slightly weird (_but near and dear to my heart, of course_) news about a revival fork of Gridsome (_good old Vue-based static site generator_) [called Gridmix](https://gridmix.github.io/blog/2026/06/12/say-hello-to-gridmix/). This is a personal project of mine and I'll probably write more about that later but for now — let me know if that rings a bell in your heart too.

You see, this fortnight was quite rich for bright bets, wins, and loses.
