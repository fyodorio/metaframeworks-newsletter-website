[🎵 _Didn't have a care, working for peanuts, not a dime to spare, but I was lean and solid everywhere..._ 🎵](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fMIJuuk1SFs&list=PLYRq_7Yox1jDETeL_YgKUc8DXduCV9jA2&index=52)

While many people think and discuss if we need metaframeworks at all and if they bring joy or contamination into the webdev world, other people work hard and thoughtfully on creating efficient and robust solutions for full-stack web development challenges. We will look at this eternal party from different sides today and everyone will (_again_) be able to see for their own.

## The Good

SolidStart team has stabilized [version 2](https://github.com/solidjs/solid-start/releases/tag/%40solidjs/start%402.0.0) of the metaframework and published [the official migration guide](https://docs.solidjs.com/solid-start/v2/migrating-from-v1). [The announcement](https://github.com/solidjs/solid-start/discussions/2281) mentiones that this update is mostly the architectural switch to Vite ecosystem and Rolldown-powered toolchain (_and [Vite's Environment API](https://vite.dev/guide/api-environment-frameworks) most importantly, enabling versatile options for targets like client, server, server functions, and whatnot, as well as more straightforward deployment setups_) throwing away unnecessary additional abstractions.

> Solid v2 is moving forward quickly. It represents the next generation of Solid, with an exciting new reactive foundation and new possibilities for the framework and its ecosystem. We are incredibly excited about where it is headed, and we will have much more to share about it soon. With the modernization work completed in SolidStart v2, we can carry this foundation forward as we move towards Solid v2 in future releases.

Interestingly, even Angular officially switched to using Rolldown by default starting in Angular v22.1.0 for production and server chunk optimization. This step is a huge shift for conservative Angular's toolset toward a Rust-powered build pipeline, moving further away from conventional and habitual Webpack.

## The Bad

Meanwhile, people get more and more intimidated by complexity of the modern webdev ecosystem (_not everyone likes even neatly engineered but overly bloated solutions_). David Poblador has beautifully described that in [his piece on frontend descend](https://davidpoblador.com/deep-dives/what-happened-to-the-frontend/).

> Somebody hit a genuine problem, built a fix, and the fix created the next problem, which got its own fix, and the sum of two decades of reasonable steps is the magnificent, exhausting cathedral of madness you're staring up at today.

Ollie Williams [seconded some parts of that](https://olliewilliams.xyz/blog/no-build/) (_unintentionally but not least persuasively_) focusing specifically on frontend build tools and their role (or rather lack of it) in the modern web development.

> Frontend build tools have improved over time, yet developer satisfaction with them hasn’t changed since 2016, remaining at 3.6/5. Configuration, excessive complexity, and performance rank as the top build-tool pain points. When asked “What aspects of JavaScript do you struggle with the most?”, dealing with build tools ranked higher than async code, security and error handling.

And even from the practical standpoint, there's a consistent tendency to simplifying the stack of technologies for building websites, with recent shiny example being [TanStack team getting rid of React Server Components](https://tanstack.com/blog/we-stopped-using-rsc-on-tanstack-com) on their official website.

> Maybe there are many more use cases where that benefit justifies the machinery and we haven't run into them yet. I'm happy to be proven wrong, but our strongest RSC use case disappeared as soon as the dependency got small, and I don't think that's nothing.

## The Noteworthy

Another interesting and long-anticipated website rebuild story is [the news about AnalogJS rebuilding their official website](https://bsky.app/profile/brandonroberts.dev/post/3msipeaytwc25) in... AnalogJS, marking important dogfooding milestone which is a good signal for people who want to use the framework (_I personally consider it musthave for any metaframework and the Minimal Viable Validation in fact_). The new metaframework version 2.7.0 brings [a lot of awesomeness](https://github.com/analogjs/analog/releases/tag/v2.7.0) to support that.

Next.js team didn't rebuild anything but they kinda called all the stars aboard for [the new minor version announcement](https://nextjs.org/blog/next-16-3) (_which is probably the most interesting part of this announcement, to be honest_). There are lots of technical improvements that worth checking out and multiple DX and AX enhancement which will make many consumers happier and lots of consumed system resources cheaper.

Astro team just [did the same](https://astro.build/blog/astro-720/) (_with less AI fanciness, thanks JavaScript gods!_) and also dropped [some news from the passed July](https://astro.build/blog/whats-new-july-2026/) which is a great read, as usual.

Important technical releases with performance improvements delivered Quasar team with [version 2.24.0](https://github.com/quasarframework/quasar/releases/tag/quasar-v2.24.0), and Hono team with [version 4.13.0](https://github.com/honojs/hono/releases/tag/v4.13.0). Releases [1.6.0](https://github.com/redwoodjs/sdk/releases/tag/v1.6.0) and [1.7.0](https://github.com/redwoodjs/sdk/releases/tag/v1.7.0) for RedwoodSDK brought multiple security fixes for the metaframework along with a bunch of technical improvements.

Svelte community, actively working on new major version of SvelteKit, at the same time actively prepares to [November Svelte Summit](https://sveltesummit.com) in Slovenian Ljubljana, so if thought the ticket price may bite, you should definitely check out this beautiful place with such a nice occasion.

As you can see, there's still a lot to get to know and a lot to do by the end of this summer, and even more to prepare to before the fall begins. So take your time, choose your weapon, and let's get rolling on through this awesome metaframeworks web development journey.
