[🎵 _When darkness comes and pain is all around, like a bridge over troubled water I will lay me down..._ 🎵](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WrcwRt6J32o&list=PLYRq_7Yox1jDETeL_YgKUc8DXduCV9jA2&index=54)

So Metaframeworks Records are back with the first issue since the cadence switch I [had announced](https://metaframe.works/archive/52/#:~:text=I%20plan%20to%20publish%20the%20issue%20#53%20closer%20to%20the%20end%20of%20September) recently. The list of news is unexpectedly short but hopegivingly interesting. The main agenda today is that people are tired of ubiquitous unsolicited AI discourse and came back to talking about frameworks and metaframeworks.

> Did 1 video on AI w/Jev, 0 comments

> I guess some people still want to talk about the web 🤔

Frameworks and metaframeworks are probably the bridges over troubled AI degradation waters — the harness to keep our creations robust (_yeah, it all is not just about a couple of markdown files [as someone thinks](https://htmx.org/essays/markdown-in-src/)_). If the open-source tools won't give up and won't altogether get absorbed by huge monsters of the enterprize world, which we'll somewhat talk about today too, there's still a huge hope that the fullstack web development is in good hands of metaframeworks. Let's dive right in.

## The Good

Ryan Carniato does just that (_dives right into the ocean of modern versatile web development tools_) in his latest piece called (_not without a certain pathos, as usual_) ["The Grand Unifying Architecture of Frontend"](https://dev.to/playfulprogramming/the-grand-unifying-architecture-of-frontend-bhk) and the accompanying [6-hour livestream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_D-n0R2miSM) on this and adjacent topics. He comes up with the matrix of the main approaches in the ecosystem based on tool responsibilities and mechanics. Not surprisingly, most modern innovation over the last few years is collapsing toward the center of this matrix as individual frameworks expand their capabilities to cover multiple quadrants simultaneously.

From Ryan's livestream I got to know the sad news about his namesake's Ryan Florence (_the exceptionally clever guy that co-created Remix and React Router alongside Michael Jackson_) recent personal departure from working on Remix in Shopify. The ongoing development of the metaframework (_whose revamp was [recently announced to be almost ready](https://remix.run/blog/remix-3-release-candidate) for public release_) remains in the hands of the Remix and React Router (_which got its [new 8.4 release](https://github.com/remix-run/react-router/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md#v840) just recently too_) engineering teams at Shopify. And the latter is moving quite fast with its CEO's hysterical stance on AI development and new eager acquisitions, [the most recent of which was Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/blog/tailwind-is-joining-shopify), the extremely popular tool most full-stack developers prefer to use for consuming CSS esotericism.

Arguably, acquisitions are never good for community and actual product development, thought there are definitely some exceptions. For instance, VoidZero slowed down the hype train after [being acquired](https://metaframe.works/archive/47/#:~:text=the%20VoidZero%20acquisition%20deal.) and from the first sight it looks bad but from the second look — metaframework authors got some time to actually work instead of never-ending chains of Vite migrations. With that, the team proceeds delivering cool stuff too. Vite+ got its [first major version](https://voidzero.dev/posts/announcing-vite-plus-1-0) (_which means proper stability_) and Vitest got [version 5](https://vitest.dev/blog/vitest-5.html) with performance improvements I personally waited so much. Moreover, Nuxt dev tools are actively [being combined](https://bsky.app/profile/antfu.me/post/3mwcpmbujd22s) with Vue dev tools which makes Nuxt developers' life so much easier.

React also got its (quite rare these days) [update 19.3](https://react.dev/blog/2026/09/09/react-19-3) bringing some polishing to the language of the main modern web UIs. Its ubiquity and robustness allows some stories, like [Evil Martians' smooth migration from Gatsby to Astro](https://astro.build/case-studies/evil-martians/), to exist and finish with happy ending, which is due in no small part to Astro itself whose team did accomplish a lot of cool stuff in both [August](https://astro.build/blog/whats-new-august-2026/) and [September](https://astro.build/blog/astro-730/) (_and not even only for CLoudflare_).

## The Bad

But you know who also can brag about its accomplishments lately? The September's [birthday-celebrating "person"](https://socket.dev/blog/happy-birthday-shai-hulud) known as Shai Hulud. It's terror spans across 12 months in a row already.

> A year after the first compromise, Shai-Hulud belongs to no one and everyone with a GitHub account.

The ecosystem still gets surprise by [new and new](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/malicious-npm-packages-evade-install-script-defenses-at-runtime/) attacks regularly. Even the attempts to protect from that on regular basis get compromised themselves, and here we are — the Next.js publishes its [urgent security release](https://nextjs.org/blog/nextjs-security-update-september-22-2026) and [prepares to a next one](https://nextjs.org/blog/upcoming-nextjs-security-release-september-2026) right away. Is it [AI to blame](https://nextjs.org/blog/how-we-closed-1500-github-issues) for this and similar issues? No one knows, but these problems evade many tools, like the recent vulnerability [findinds in Hono](https://github.com/honojs/hono/releases/tag/v4.13.11).

The community persistently tries to stand for the web safety with new initiatives though, like the recent [OpenJS Foundation's Security Stewardship Program](https://openjsf.org/blog/openjs-foundation-launches-the-security-stewardshi) for OSS. Any help like that bring a good hope for the future of web development in the context of modern open-source tools like metaframeworks.

> The JavaScript ecosystem processes more npm package downloads per week than any other package registry. The surface area for vulnerabilities is enormous, and the maintainer population doing the hardest security work is small and largely volunteer. [...] AI is driving a sharp rise in vulnerability reports, pushing many open source projects to shut down their bug bounty programs. The bottleneck is now remediation. [...] Finding vulnerabilities is only part of the job. Someone still has to triage them, fix them, and get secure releases into the hands of developers. [...] Open source powers the world's software, and the people maintaining it deserve more than goodwill.
